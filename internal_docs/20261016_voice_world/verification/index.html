1. What was built

Conjure is a first-person 3D world in desktop Chrome that you change by talking. You say a sentence and something new falls into the world, in a spot that makes sense, in about 1.5 s. "Take me to X" builds a whole place around you in about 10 s. Models never draw. The fast intent model (Haiku 5.5) turns each sentence into small JSON ops: selectors for sets, counts and arrangements, and define for anything not in the kit. A Sonnet Director streams scene recipes for teleports. A deterministic engine handles placement, geometry and the arrival animation. Voice runs through gpt-realtime-mini on the AI Gateway with build and teleport tools, and an optional webcam handles head-look and pointing. Kyle set the north star on Oct 17: "I want the player to almost feel like they're god or they have a genie. It should be a 'holy shit' experience." Every improvement loop was scored against that, not against "works correctly": fast responses that seem to anticipate you, spectacular arrivals, and a world that feels alive and answers to you.

3. Sprints 1–5

Sprint 1: World kernel and kit

A first-person world you change by typing raw JSON ops: the WorldOp contract in shared/world/ops.ts, a selector engine, a layout engine (cluster, line, ring, grid, scatter, along), 12 kit prefabs plus learned kinds from parts, arrival animations, an op log with undo per batch, and localStorage save and replay.

Notable deviations

  • Unknown kinds spawn a labelled placeholder that keeps the requested name, so a later define morphs every placeholder in place.
  • undo replays the log minus the last batch; no-op batches aren't logged, so undo always undoes something visible.
  • Selector leniency: plurals and kind falling back to a tag (kind: 'tree' finds pines and oaks).

Sprint 2: The op brain (text)

Plain English in, ops out: /api/intent on Haiku 5.5 with a cached ~8k-token digest, a text bar, an op log, and bench/intent.ts (34 utterances × 8 scenes, applied with the real applyBatch and checked on the resulting state).

Sprint 3: Voice

Realtime voice through the gateway with one build({request}) tool. The client attaches the gaze snapshot taken at speech-started, so "there" means where you were looking when you said it. The spike confirmed the gateway forwards function calls for gpt-realtime-mini, so V1 is the main path, with browser speech as the fallback.

Notable deviations

  • No custom audio class: ai@7.0.143 creates audio lazily in connect(), so Camera Shy's seam didn't apply. The SDK audio gets our own getUserMedia stream.
  • No session-update after connect, so no baseConfig; tools come only from the token route.
  • VAD silence 600 ms (later 400 ms). One of 4 runs missed the first sentence and didn't reproduce.

Sprint 4: Teleport mode (Director and atmosphere)

"Take me to X" goes to a Sonnet Director that streams a scene recipe in build order. The engine plays each op as it arrives, outward from the player, while the fog recedes. Added 28 interior, outdoor and city kinds plus synthesized ambient sound beds.

Sprint 5: Hands-free (webcam)

MediaPipe Face Landmarker for head-look (dead zone ±4°, direct offset to 15°, then a turn rate) and Hand Landmarker for pointing as a gaze override. Lazy-loaded only when the camera toggle is pressed. 19 tracking tests.

Not verified: turning and pointing with a real head and hand. A still image can't fake a head turn; the rate band is covered only by unit tests. See needs a human.

  • Pointing reticle is a DOM ring; pointing at the sky still falls back to 14 m ahead along the camera yaw.
  • Lean-to-walk moved to Later.

4. Improvement loop 1 (overall 4/10)

The evaluator played 17 + 10 typed commands and 2 spoken ones on swiftshader. Teleports earned an "oh, nice". The two biggest wishes produced the two ugliest frames.

Top awe-killers

  1. Results land out of frame, inside the camera, or too small to matter. Huge dragon: camera between its legs. Giant tower: 3 m away, top cut off. 10×: black screen.
  2. Nothing moves, and nothing comes from the sky. No altitude, no motion, no weather. Lanterns sat on the floor, and the say line claimed it worked.
  3. Arrivals are polite, not divine. The same 7 m drop for a chair and a castle; "ready" pill during a 10 s Director build.

What changed

  • Immersive HUD: an orb with state-driven colour and motion, serif captions, a hidden wish field (type-to-start), dev mode behind backtick, and a summoning phase during Director builds.
  • Framing and spectacle: size-aware placement (fitDistance), a camera glide that frames every wish, tiered arrivals (sparkle → dust → sky beam + shockwave + shake), synthesized sfx, a light-tunnel vortex for teleports, lamp light following lamp colour, and a god view for global scale-ups.
  • Brain and voice: intent moved from Output.object (served by Bedrock) to streamed text-JSON pinned to Anthropic, with NDJSON per op. The Director uses the same scanner and the grammar warm-up was deleted. A realtime teleport tool, VAD 400 ms, honest tool results, and a genie persona (voice cedar) that speaks a 1–3 word acknowledgement first.

Latency (loop1_latency.md and loop1_changes.md)

The latency study found /api/intent was being served by Bedrock in tool mode, because Anthropic refused the 49-optional schema and gateway affinity overrode order. Only only: ['anthropic'] pins a provider.

5. Improvement loop 2 (overall 5/10)

Arrival spectacle jumped +3 and immersion +4. Voice teleport became a real "oh wow". But everything that should be the climax was still static, refused, or lost to the camera.

Top awe-killers

  1. The sky and motion half of wishes still did nothing. 5 weather wishes, 2 flights, a gallop, an eruption and 3 lantern wishes all got "not yet".
  2. The camera fought you. The framing dolly moved the player 22, 40, then 100 m, sometimes through walls; after 10× and undo you stayed 40–160 m up.
  3. Typing broke the first word. Type-to-start ate leading W, A, S, D, M and space: "a dragon" was sent as "ragon", and M toggled mute.

What changed

  • Camera, input and anticipation: W/A/S/D/M/space are "maybe typing" for 350 ms. The dolly is capped at 7 m with a wall stop, otherwise it tilts ("awe from below"). The god view records and restores your pose. A gathering glow while you speak or type, and a mote placeholder spindle after 700 ms or on a define. A 19-part dragon example.
  • Terrain and scale: mountain, mountain_range, hill, cliff, volcano, waterfall, river, island, canyon, giant_moon, planet; aerial perspective; far plane 3200 m; anchor: 'horizon' and dir: 'around'; terrain snap; a Director example for 360° cities.
  • Sky, motion and weather: altitude, motion (hover, drift, orbit, fly, wander, gallop, spin, sway), weather (rain, snow, storm, fog, aurora), events (lightning, meteors, eclipse, sunrise), playerScale, glow on everything, lantern and bird kinds, and an honesty filter that strips claims the ops didn't make.
  • Integration: walking rides terrain height, mesh-only raycasters, interiors cap airborne spawns under the ceiling, and a castle on a missing hill raises the hill first.

6. Improvement loop 3 (overall 7/10)

The first loop on the real GPU, so frame-time numbers mean something. Scale and aliveness each jumped +4. The 60-second demo still broke on 3 of its 6 beats, all for small, fixable reasons.

Top awe-killers

  1. "Make me tiny" on a dock went under the deck: the rest of the demo was a brown screen.
  2. A missing subject was refused: "Summon a dragon to fly around me" got "Alas, nothing here answers to 'dragon'. Shall I summon one?" (a butler, not a genie).
  3. Sky arrivals weren't framed: only 1–4 of 50 lanterns were in frame at eye level.
  4. Weather reset the time of day (a storm flipped night to dusk), and Director builds hitched down to 2 fps with 988 ms frames.

What changed

  • Intent and honesty: rule 12 (a named missing thing gets spawned), a ban on offers, a kernel conjure fallback (a modify of a missing known kind becomes a spawn), keepTime so weather never sets the time unless asked, and the honesty filter on every path with effectLine replacing fragments.
  • Performance: a fixed LightPool (6 point + 6 spot lights always present, kit lights become sources on layer 31) so the light count never changes and nothing recompiles; warmKit precompiles 497 materials via compileAsync after load; per-sync precompile of hidden holders.
  • Camera and sky: standAt rides walkable structures (dock, floor, rug, road); skyLook tilts toward sky flocks and storms; a skyDome layout puts lanterns 10–35° above the eye; indoor floaters scaled 0.45× with a halo budget; storm strikes at 0.7 s then 2.6–3.8 s; readable night snow.
  • Creatures: a hand-built kit dragon (16 m, 19 m wingspan, jointed membrane wings, embers), a 25 m sky-whale with flank lights, horse and deer on one articulated quadruped, a fish school and butterflies, all rigged and animated in motion.ts, with dive and sink arrivals plus sfxDragon and sfxWhaleSong.

7. End-to-end: the final demo (overall 8.5/10)

Played on the real GPU as one continuous voice session (a fake-mic WAV of six utterances about 10 s apart), then two typed wishes in the same page. A frame was captured every 2 s and a rAF tracer logged frame times. 8/8 beats did what was asked, with 7 voice tool calls, no errors, and 60 fps on every beat. Spend was about $0.30.

Integration fixes before the run

  • Kit creatures in the digest: dragon, whale, horse and similar are kit spawns, never defines. 4 new bench cases.
  • The view (yaw, pitch, x, z) is saved on pagehide and unmount, so a remount no longer resets it after "make me tiny".
  • No stepping out of rooms: under a ceiling, framing only turns and tilts (maxBack: 0).
  • Teleports arrive level: beginReveal eases pitch into [−0.2, 0.1] during the vortex (found in this demo).

8. Remaining issues and human checks

Remaining issues (ranked by what they cost the demo)

  1. The sky tilt never eases back. After the lanterns, pitch sits at 0.55–0.62, so ground-level arrivals are half out of frame. Fix in renderer.ts skyLook/frameWish.
  2. Sky arrivals at the frame edge. skyTilt returns null when you already look high enough, so the whale needed about 9 s to swim into view (fx/frame.ts).
  3. Sound is still unverified (thunder, whale song, dragon roar, rain bed): checked for graph errors only.
  4. The mountain beat is redundant after a Director place that already has a range. Keep the horizon open, or swap in "raise a volcano over there".
  5. Director teleports take 8–11 s (city: first op 2.5 s, done 11.4 s). Hitch-free now, but long.
  6. The city caption doubled: the Director's say and the voice line both get captioned (useDirector.ts).
  7. The teleport-level fix needs a voice re-run. It was verified only on a typed teleport, and it changes beginReveal for every teleport.
  8. Tiny needs close-up props (grass blades, a pebble) to sell scale on open ground.
  9. Indoors, framing still tilts up toward hanging lamps (about 0.27 rad). Minor.
  10. One flaky bench case ("make the dragon fly in circles" in a grove → modify ref:gaze). Not yet tested on a weaker GPU.

Needs a human

  • Listen to the sound. Play the recommended demo once with headphones: ambient beds, arrival sfx, thunder, rain, terrain rumble, the dragon's roar, the whale song and the genie voice (cedar). Note levels and timing. M mutes.
  • Real-webcam head tracking. Check the signs: turning your head left should turn the view left, and tilting up should look up (otherwise flip HEAD_SIGN in app/world/tracking/math.ts). Then check how the gains, the ±4° dead zone and the 15° turn-rate band feel, the pointing thresholds on a real hand (POINT_TUNING), and the GPU-delegate start time (13 s cold on SwiftShader).
  • VAD 400 ms with hesitant speech. Every voice test used say fixtures. Speak "put trees… over there" with natural pauses; if sentences get clipped, raise silenceDurationMs to 500 in shared/voice.ts.
